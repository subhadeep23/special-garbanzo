The 0/0 toolkit

Four moves clear an indeterminate form. She chooses by what she sees, not by trial and error.

Why this lesson. Lesson 2 taught her to read the signal. This lesson is what she does after the signal. Most of the algebraic limit marks in the ISC paper are here. The lesson is longer than the others, so split it across two sittings if needed: moves 1 and 2 first, then moves 3 and 4.

Say it simply

A 00 form means the top and the bottom both vanish at x = a. Two expressions that vanish at the same place share a factor. That shared factor is what hides the answer. Find it, cancel it, and the answer is simply sitting there.

All four moves do the one job: expose the shared factor. They differ only in how the expression is dressed.

The same idea in her kitchen

The recipe is written badly. It says "take nothing, and share it between nobody". That is not a wrong recipe — it is a recipe that has not been written out properly yet. She rewrites the recipe. She does not change the dish.

The top and the bottom both vanish at x = a because they contain the same ingredient. That shared ingredient sits on both sides of the line and cancels out. Take it out of the written recipe, and the dish is unchanged, but now she can taste it.

MoveWhat she seesIn the kitchen
1 FactorisePolynomial over polynomial Split both sides into their ingredients, then remove the one they share
2 xn − anA large or fractional power The same removal, done by a formula instead of by hand, because splitting it by hand would take all evening
3 RationaliseA square root The shared ingredient is trapped under the root. Multiply by the conjugate to bring it out where she can reach it
4 LCMTwo fractions subtracted Two half-written recipes. Put them on one line first, then look again
Why the cancelling is honest, in her words

She never actually uses the exact amount a. She only creeps up on it. So the shared ingredient is never truly zero while she works — it is a tiny amount, and dividing by a tiny amount is completely allowed. Cancelling is not a trick. It is the same dish, written in a form she can taste.

Why cancelling is legal — do not skip this

She will cancel (x − 2) from top and bottom. Her instinct says that is cheating, because at x = 2 that factor is zero, and dividing by zero is forbidden. She is right to be suspicious. Here is the answer.

The reason, in one sentence

Inside a limit, x approaches 2 but never equals 2. So (x − 2) is never zero during the whole process. It is a small non-zero number. Dividing by a small non-zero number is completely legal.

This is the deepest idea in the unit and it takes 60 seconds. Lesson 1 already built it: a limit never looks at the point itself. So the two functions

x2 − 4x − 2   and   x + 2

are the same function everywhere except at one single point, x = 2. The first has a hole there; the second does not. A limit cannot tell them apart, because a limit never inspects that point. So they have the same limit, and the second one is easy.

Ask her this out loud, and make her answer it: "Why may you cancel (x − 2)?" The answer must be "because x is never equal to 2." Not "because it cancels".

She looks, she names the move, then she writes. The naming takes two seconds and it saves the whole question. A sum that needs two moves is common — take the LCM, and then factorise what appears.

1

Factorise and cancel

Trigger: both the top and the bottom are polynomials.

Worked example 1

limx→2 x2 − 4x2 − 5x + 6

Step 1 — substitute, to read the signal.

= 4 − 44 − 10 + 6 = 00  —  work needed

Step 2 — factorise both. The shared factor must be (x − 2), because x = 2 is what made each one zero.

= limx→2 (x − 2)(x + 2)(x − 2)(x − 3)

Step 3 — cancel, because x ≠ 2 throughout.

= limx→2 x + 2x − 3

Step 4 — substitute again. Now the bottom is not zero.

= 4−1 = −4

The shortcut that finds the factor instantly

If substituting x = a makes an expression zero, then (x − a) is a factor of it. That is the factor theorem, and it means she never has to search. The approach point tells her the factor before she starts. x → 2 means look for (x − 2), every time.

For a cubic, use the factor theorem then divide. Example: x3 − 8 has x = 2 as a root, so (x − 2) is a factor, giving (x − 2)(x2 + 2x + 4). Keep the standard identities to hand:

a2 − b2 = (a−b)(a+b)   ·   a3 − b3 = (a−b)(a2+ab+b2)   ·   a3 + b3 = (a+b)(a2−ab+b2)

2

The xn − an form

Trigger: a power too large to factorise by hand, or a fractional power, sitting over (x − a).

The standard result — learn it exactly

limx→a xn − anx − a = n an−1

This holds for every real n, not only whole numbers.2 It is move 1 done in advance and stored as a formula.

Worked example 2 — a large power

limx→2 x5 − 32x − 2

Recognise 32 = 25. So this is the standard form with n = 5 and a = 2.

= 5 · 24 = 80

Factorising x5 − 32 by hand would take five minutes and invite errors. The formula takes five seconds.

Worked example 3 — a fractional power

limx→1 x3/2 − 1x − 1   with n = 32, a = 1

= 32 · 11/2 = 32

The extended form — worth 30 seconds of memory

limx→a xn − anxm − am = nm an−m

Divide the top and the bottom by (x − a) and apply the standard result twice. She should be able to derive it in one line, and that is better than memorising it.

3

Rationalise with the conjugate

Trigger: a square root anywhere in the expression.

Factorising cannot reach inside a root. So remove the root first. Multiply the top and the bottom by the conjugate — the same expression with the middle sign flipped. The identity (a−b)(a+b) = a2−b2 then squares the root away.

Worked example 4

limx→0 √x + 4 − 2x

Step 1 — substitute: (2 − 2)/0 = 0/0. Work needed. A root is present, so rationalise.

Step 2 — multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the top.

= limx→0 (√x+4 − 2)(√x+4 + 2)x(√x+4 + 2)

Step 3 — the top is now a difference of squares: (x+4) − 4 = x.

= limx→0 xx(√x+4 + 2)

Step 4 — the shared factor x has appeared. Cancel it, because x ≠ 0.

= limx→0 1√x+4 + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14

Two things she will get wrong here

1. Do not expand the bottom. Leave it as x(√(x+4) + 2). The whole point is to keep the factor x visible so it can cancel. A student who multiplies the bottom out destroys the very thing she was working towards.

2. Rationalise the side that has the root. If the root is on the bottom, multiply by the conjugate of the bottom. If roots are on both sides, do one side, cancel, then look again.

4

Take the LCM first

Trigger: two fractions subtracted, so substitution gives ∞ − ∞ rather than 0/0.

∞ − ∞ is also indeterminate, and it is not something she can work with. Combine the two fractions into one, and the form usually turns into 0/0. Then use move 1.

Worked example 5 — two moves chained

limx→1 1x − 1 − 2x2 − 1

Step 1 — substitute: 1/0 − 2/0, which is ∞ − ∞. Not usable. Take the LCM.

Step 2 — factorise the second denominator to find the LCM: x2 − 1 = (x−1)(x+1). So the LCM is (x−1)(x+1).

= limx→1 (x + 1) − 2(x − 1)(x + 1)

Step 3 — simplify the top. It becomes x − 1, which is the shared factor.

= limx→1 x − 1(x − 1)(x + 1) = limx→1 1x + 1 = 12

Notice the shape of the work. The LCM converted ∞ − ∞ into 0/0, and then move 1 finished it. Chaining two moves is normal.

Exercise set

  1. Easy Evaluate limx→5 x2−25x2−7x+10.
    Hint 1 — the typePolynomial over polynomial. Move 1.
    Hint 2 — the moveThe approach point is 5, so (x−5) is a factor of both.
    Answer = (x−5)(x+5) / (x−5)(x−2) = (x+5)/(x−2) → 10/3. Answer 10/3.
  2. Easy Evaluate limx→3 x6−729x−3.
    Hint 1 — the typeA sixth power. Move 2. Ask what 729 is.
    Answer 729 = 36, so n = 6 and a = 3. Answer = 6 · 35 = 6 · 243 = 1458.
  3. Medium Evaluate limx→0 √1+x − √1−xx.
    Hint 1 — the typeTwo roots on top. Move 3.
    Hint 2 — the moveThe conjugate of √(1+x) − √(1−x) is √(1+x) + √(1−x).
    Hint 3 — the first lineThe new top is (1+x) − (1−x) = 2x. Keep the bottom unexpanded.
    Answer = 2x / [x(√(1+x) + √(1−x))] = 2 / (√(1+x) + √(1−x)) → 2/(1+1) = 1.
  4. Medium Evaluate limx→1 x4−1x3−1.
    Hint 1 — the typeBoth top and bottom are of the xn−an shape, but neither denominator is (x−1) on its own.
    Hint 2 — the moveDivide the top and the bottom by (x−1), then take each limit separately. Or use the extended form.
    Answer Divide top and bottom by (x−1):
    = [(x4−1)/(x−1)] ÷ [(x3−1)/(x−1)] → (4 · 13) / (3 · 12) = 4/3.
    By the extended form: (n/m)an−m = (4/3) · 11 = 4/3.
  5. Hard Evaluate limx→3 √x+1 − 2x2 − 9.
    Hint 1 — the typeTwo moves. A root is present, so rationalise first, then factorise.
    Hint 2 — the moveMultiply by √(x+1) + 2. Factorise the bottom as (x−3)(x+3) at the same time.
    Hint 3 — the first lineThe new top is (x+1) − 4 = x − 3. That is the shared factor.
    Answer = (x−3) / [(x−3)(x+3)(√(x+1)+2)]
    = 1 / [(x+3)(√(x+1)+2)] → 1/(6 × 4) = 1/24.
    The order matters. Rationalise first, always, because the root blocks the factorising.
  6. Hard Find a and b if limx→1 a x2 + b x − 5x − 1 = 7.
    Hint 1 — the typeWork backwards. The bottom is 0 at x = 1, and the limit exists, so what must the top be?
    Hint 2 — the moveCondition 1: the top must be zero at x = 1, giving one equation. Then factorise and apply the limit for the second equation.
    Hint 3 — the first linea + b − 5 = 0, so b = 5 − a.
    Answer Since the limit exists and the bottom → 0, the top must also → 0:
    a + b − 5 = 0, so b = 5 − a.  (1)
    Substitute: top = ax2 + (5−a)x − 5. At x = 1 it is zero, so (x−1) is a factor:
    top = (x−1)(ax + 5).
    So the limit = limx→1(ax+5) = a + 5 = 7, giving a = 2 and b = 3.
    Check: (2x2+3x−5)/(x−1) = (x−1)(2x+5)/(x−1) → 7. Correct.
  7. Stretch Evaluate limx→0 1x [1√1+x − 1].
    Hint 1 — the typeBoth a root and a difference of fractions. Simplify the bracket first.
    Hint 2 — the moveThe bracket becomes (1 − √(1+x)) / √(1+x). Now rationalise that numerator.
    Answer Bracket = (1 − √(1+x)) / √(1+x).
    Multiply the numerator by its conjugate (1 + √(1+x)): numerator becomes 1 − (1+x) = −x.
    So the whole expression = −x / [x · √(1+x) · (1 + √(1+x))]. Cancel x:
    = −1 / [√(1+x)(1 + √(1+x))] → −1/(1 × 2) = −1/2.
    Sanity check on the sign: for small positive x, √(1+x) is slightly more than 1, so 1/√(1+x) is slightly less than 1, so the bracket is negative. A negative bracket divided by a positive x is negative. The answer must be negative, and it is.
Close the lesson — she says this, not you

"0/0 means the top and the bottom share a factor. I choose the move by what I see: polynomials means factorise, a big or fractional power over (x−a) means the formula, a root means rationalise, and two fractions means LCM. I may cancel the shared factor because x never equals a."

Her kitchen version: "The top and the bottom share an ingredient. I take that shared ingredient out of the written recipe. The dish does not change — only the way it is written changes — and now I can taste it."

Ask. The most common place to get stuck is choosing between move 1 and move 2. If she is unsure, either will work on a small power — move 2 is only a time-saver. Ask before she spends fifteen minutes factorising x7 − 128 by hand.

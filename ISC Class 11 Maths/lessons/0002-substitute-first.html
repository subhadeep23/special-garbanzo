Substitute first, then read the signal

Every limit sum begins the same way. Put the number in. What comes out tells you what to do next.

Why this lesson. Most limits in the ISC paper need no technique at all — substitution finishes them. The rest announce which technique they need, and they announce it in a code she can learn in one sitting. This is the shortest lesson in the pack and it prevents more lost marks than any other. The board asks for it directly: "Indeterminate forms are to be introduced while calculating limits."1

Say it simply

Never start a limit by looking for a trick. Put the number in first. Then look at what you got. The result is a signal, and there are only three signals.

The same idea in her kitchen

Before she reaches for any technique, she puts the ingredient in the recipe and tastes it. The taste tells her what to do next.

Substitution givesIn the kitchenWhat she does
A number The recipe works. The dish has a taste Stop. That is the answer
non-zero0 The recipe divides the dish between zero people. That has no meaning, and no amount of cooking repairs it Stop. The limit does not exist
00 Nothing on top, nothing on the bottom. The recipe says nothing at all yet Work. Rewrite the recipe first

The word "indeterminate" in her words: the recipe has not told her the taste yet. It has not refused. It has not answered. She must rewrite the recipe into a form that can answer, and Lesson 3 gives the four ways.

Why substitution is allowed at all

She may substitute because of the limit laws. The syllabus asks for these as statements only — she states them and uses them, she never proves them.1 That saves a session. Spend it on practice.

Suppose limx→af(x) = L and limx→ag(x) = M. Then:

LawStatementIn plain words
Sum lim[f + g] = L + M Take the limit of each piece, then add
Difference lim[f − g] = L − M Same, then subtract
Constant multiple lim[k f] = k L A constant walks straight through
Product lim[f · g] = L · M Same, then multiply
Quotient limfg = LM,  only if M ≠ 0 Same, then divide. The condition is the whole lesson
Power lim[f]n = Ln The power waits outside
Constant limx→ak = k A constant does not move
Identity limx→ax = a x heads to a. Obviously
What the laws add up to

Put the constant law, the identity law, and the sum, product, and power laws together. They give this, for every polynomial:

limx→ap(x) = p(a)

For a polynomial, substitution is the method. It is not a shortcut and it is not an approximation. It is the answer, and it earns full marks. For a rational function it works too, and for the same reason — as long as the denominator is not zero. Look at the condition on the quotient law again. Every hard limit in this unit is a limit where that one condition fails.

The decision tree

Substitute x = a. Exactly one of these three happens.

1

You get a number. That number is the answer. Stop.

limx→2 x2 + 3x + 1 = 4 + 33 = 73

Write the answer and move to the next question. Around half the limit marks in the paper are here. She loses them by hunting for a technique that the sum does not need.

Zero on top is still a number. limx→3 x − 3x2 + 1 = 010 = 0. The answer is 0, and the sum is finished. This case catches almost everyone, because a zero appeared and she assumed trouble. Zero on top is an answer. Zero on the bottom is the trouble.

2

You get 00. This is a signal. Do algebra.

limx→1 x2 − 1x − 1 = 00  —  indeterminate

Indeterminate means "not yet determined". It does not mean impossible, and it does not mean zero, and it does not mean one. It means the sum has not started. Something on the top and something on the bottom both vanish at x = a, which means they share a common factor, and that factor is hiding the answer. Cancel it and the answer appears.

Here the answer is 2. Lesson 3 gives the four moves that get it.

Proof that 00 cannot be a number. Show her these three, together:

limx→03xx = 3   ·   limx→0xx = 1   ·   limx→0x2x = 0

All three substitute to 00. The answers are 3, 1, and 0. So the form 00 carries no information about the answer, and that is exactly what "indeterminate" means. Once she sees this, she stops writing 00 = 1.

3

You get non-zero0. The limit does not exist.

limx→3 x2 + 1x − 3 = 100  —  does not exist

This form is not indeterminate. It is fully determined, and what it determines is failure. A fixed non-zero top divided by a bottom shrinking to zero grows without bound. There is no finite flavour to name, so there is no limit.

No algebra helps here, and she must not look for any. There is no common factor to cancel, because the top is not zero. Recognising this saves her five minutes of hunting per sum.

For extra marks she may describe the two sides: from the right the values rise to +∞, from the left they fall to −∞. Then state that the limit does not exist.

The three mistakes this lesson prevents

1. She writes 00 = 1, because "anything over itself is 1".
The real gap: the rule a/a = 1 needs a ≠ 0. It has always needed that. Zero is the case the rule excludes.

2. She sees 100 and hunts for something to factorise.
The real gap: she treats every zero denominator as the same event. Factorising can only help when top and bottom vanish together, because only then is there a shared factor.

3. She sees 010 and writes "does not exist".
The real gap: she has learned "zero means trouble" instead of "zero downstairs means trouble". Ask her: is 0 ÷ 10 a legal division? Yes. So the answer is 0.

Exercise set

  1. Easy Evaluate limx→2 x3 − 2x + 1x2 + 1.
    Hint 1 — the type Check the denominator at x = 2 before anything else.
    Answer Bottom = 5, which is not zero, so substitute. Top = 8 − 4 + 1 = 5. Answer = 1.
  2. Easy Explain why limx→5 x − 5x + 5 = 0 but limx→−5 x − 5x + 5 does not exist.
    Hint 1 — the type The same expression, two different approach points. Substitute into each separately.
    Answer At x = 5: top = 0, bottom = 10, so 0/10 = 0. A legal division.
    At x = −5: top = −10, bottom = 0, so −10/0 — the limit does not exist. It is the position of the zero that decides, never the presence of a zero.
  3. Medium For each, write only the form that substitution gives, and state whether it is determinate or indeterminate. (a) limx→0sin xx   (b) limx→0cos xx   (c) limx→0xcos x
    Hint 1 — the type Substitution practice on trigonometric functions. Recall sin 0 = 0 and cos 0 = 1.
    Hint 2 — the move Compute the top and the bottom separately, each time. Do not guess from the shape of the expression.
    Answer (a) 0/0 — indeterminate. Work needed. This is the famous one, and Lesson 4 shows the answer is 1.
    (b) 1/0 — determinate. The limit does not exist. No work is possible.
    (c) 0/1 = 0 — determinate, and finished.
    Three expressions built from the same two functions, and three completely different outcomes. This is why she substitutes first, every time.
  4. Medium A student writes: "limx→2 x2−4x−2 = 00 = 1." Find the error, and give the correct answer.
    Hint 1 — the type Error hunting. The error is in the second equals sign, not the first.
    Hint 2 — the move Which condition does the rule a/a = 1 require?
    Answer The first step is right: substitution does give 0/0. The error is treating that form as a value. The rule a/a = 1 requires a ≠ 0, so it says nothing about 0/0.
    Correct working: factorise the top as (x−2)(x+2), cancel (x−2), then substitute into x + 2. Answer = 4.
    Note that 4 ≠ 1, which proves the shortcut was wrong.
  5. Hard Find every value of k for which limx→3 x2 + kx − 3 can possibly exist.
    Hint 1 — the type Work backwards from the decision tree. The bottom is zero at x = 3, and she cannot change that.
    Hint 2 — the move The bottom is 0. So the only branch that leaves any hope is 0/0. What must the top be?
    Hint 3 — the first line Require top = 0 at x = 3:   9 + k = 0.
    Answer The bottom is always 0 at x = 3. So if the top is non-zero, the form is non-zero/0 and the limit certainly fails. The only hope is 0/0, which needs 9 + k = 0, so k = −9.
    Check it: with k = −9 the expression becomes x2−9x−3, which cancels to x + 3, and the limit is 6. So k = −9 works, and no other value can.
  6. Stretch Give an example of each: two limits that both substitute to 0/0, where the first equals 100 and the second does not exist.
    Hint 1 — the type A construction question. She must build the functions, so she must understand what 0/0 does and does not tell her.
    Hint 2 — the move For the first, put the answer she wants in the numerator as a factor. For the second, recall x/x2 and what that does at 0.
    Answer First: limx→0 100xx = 100, and substitution gives 0/0.
    Second: limx→0 xx2 = limx→0 1x, which does not exist, and substitution also gives 0/0.
    Together they prove the point of the whole lesson: 0/0 tells her that work is needed, and nothing whatever about the answer. The answer can be any number, or there may be no answer at all.
Close the lesson — she says this, not you

"I always substitute first. If I get a number, that is the answer. If I get something non-zero over zero, the limit does not exist. Only 0/0 means I have work to do, and it tells me nothing about what the answer will be."

Her kitchen version: "I taste it first. A taste is the answer. Sharing between nobody is a dead end. Nothing over nothing means the recipe has not told me anything yet, so I rewrite it."

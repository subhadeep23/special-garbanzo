Lesson 01 · ~7 minutes · cube in hand

Read any algorithm without thinking

Your goal is to solve unaided. The thing standing in the way isn't the steps — it's that the algorithms haven't stuck. And you can't memorise a sentence in a language you read letter-by-letter. So today's single win: turn notation into reflex.

Why this first Every move sequence you'll ever learn is written in six letters and three symbols. When R U R' U' reads as one chunk instead of four lookups, it moves into long-term memory as a single unit. Fluency now is what makes unaided possible later.

Step 1 — Anchor the six letters (90 seconds)

Hold your cube still. One face points at your nose. Without rotating the cube, name them out loud by position, not colour:

  • Front at your nose · Back behind · Up · Down · Left · Right.

Full table in the Notation reference — keep it open in another tab.

Step 2 — The three turn-symbols (90 seconds)

WriteSayDo
R"R"turn that face 90° clockwise (as if it faced you)
R'"R prime"90° counter-clockwise
R2"R two"180° (either way)

Step 3 — The self-checking drill (the win)

Start from a solved cube. Do the "sexy move" — the single most common pattern in cubing:

R U R' U'

Repeat that exact four-move sequence six times. If you read every move correctly, the cube returns perfectly to solved. If it doesn't, you misread a move — reset and go slower.

Your win today A cube that returns to solved after 6× R U R' U' proves you can read notation reliably. That's the whole foundation — every algorithm from here is just longer sentences in the same language.
The clockwise gotcha Clockwise is judged from that face's point of view. The sneaky ones are D (imagine looking up from underneath) and B (from behind the cube). Get these wrong and a whole solve falls apart — so drill them deliberately.

Step 4 — Recall check

Answer from memory, cube down. No peeking at the table above.

Energy — the lens that unifies all of it

Lesson 2 left one word undefined: graded. This lesson defines it. There are two formulas in here. Each one gets an everyday picture first, then the symbols named one at a time. If a formula still feels opaque, that is a fault in the lesson — tell me and I will redraw it.

1 · One number: the complaint meter

Picture it You have a friend who watches short video clips with you. You pause a clip, describe what you think happens next, and the friend gives you a complaint score. Silence, a zero, means "yes, that is how it goes." A loud score means "no, that does not follow." The friend never tells you the right answer. The friend only complains.

That friend is the whole idea. You hand the machine a pair — a past x and a candidate future y — and it returns one number. The number is the complaint. Low means the two fit together. High means they do not.

The number is called the energy. The machine is an energy function, written E(x, y). That is the entire primitive. It is not a probability. It is not a prediction. It is a fit score.

🗣️ Plain: one number that says how badly these two things fit together. Small means they fit. STATa scalar-valued function of a data pair; a fitted measure of (in)compatibility, unnormalised
CS/MLan energy function E(x, y) — the model outputs a scalar energy for each input pair

2 · Scores versus shares — and the census you cannot take

You have met this object before, under another name. Watch the translation, but read the picture first.

Picture it A talent show. The judge can do two different jobs.

Job A — give scores. "This act: 4 complaints. That act: 22 complaints." To do this, the judge only needs to watch the acts in front of them.

Job B — give each act a share of the vote. "This act gets 8% of the total." A share is a slice of a whole. So before the judge can name any slice, they must total the scores of every act that could ever walk on stage. That is a census.

A probability is Job B. An energy is Job A. Job A is easy. Job B needs the census.

Here is the same statement in symbols. This is the standard exponential form of a conditional density:

p(y | x)  =  exp( −E(x, y) )  /  Z(x)

  • p(y | x)the share of the vote: how probable this future y is, given this past x. All the shares must add up to 1.
  • E(x, y)the complaint score from section 1. Low means they fit.
  • −Ethe minus sign flips the direction. A low complaint becomes a high number, because probabilities want big-is-good.
  • exp( · )a converter. It turns a score anywhere on the number line into a positive weight, and it stretches the gaps: a slightly lower complaint becomes a much larger weight.
  • Z(x)the census. The total of exp(−E) over every possible y. Division by it turns raw weights into shares that add up to 1.

Take logs, move the terms around, and the same line reads:

E(x, y)  =  −log p(y | x)  −  log Z(x)

So energy is negative log-likelihood, apart from that census term. Maximum likelihood and minimum energy are one instinct in two coats. You minimised energy for years and called it fitting a model.

Why the census is the whole argument

Look hard at Z(x). It totals exp(−E(x, y)) over every y that can possibly occur.

  • For the next word: y is one of about 50,000 tokens. The census is a sum over 50,000 things. A softmax does it in a moment. This is why LLMs can work in probabilities at all.
  • For the next second of video: y is a point in a space of millions of continuous dimensions. The census is an integral over that whole space. Nobody can compute it. Not slowly, and not approximately enough.
Why LeCun works in energy and not probability A probability is a share, so it owes a census over every possible future. An energy is a score, so it owes nothing. It only has to be lower here than there. Refuse the duty to normalise, and prediction in a huge continuous space becomes possible.
🗣️ Plain: the census — the total you must know before you can turn any score into a percentage. It is impossible to take in a huge continuous space. STATthe normalising constant Z(x); intractable for high-dimensional continuous y
CS/MLthe partition function — energy-based models are deliberately unnormalised, which is exactly what makes them usable here

3 · The blur, finally explained at the root

Here is the payoff for Lesson 1 and Lesson 2. The pen topples. It can land left, or it can land right.

A pixel-predicting model must emit one y. Under squared error, the best single y is the average of the two — the ghost pen, the blur. The model has no way to say "either of these."

The complaint meter has no such problem. It can stay silent for pen-left and stay silent for pen-right, and complain loudly about the smeared pen between them.

The one-sentence version to memorise A predictor must name one future, so it averages them. An energy function only has to rank futures, so it can call several of them good at once — which is exactly what uncertainty needs.

4 · So what is training?

If the model is a landscape of complaints, then training is landscape sculpture. It has exactly two moves:

  • Push the energy down on pairs you saw in the data. Those futures really did follow those pasts, so they must score as compatible.
  • Push the energy up everywhere else — or the landscape goes flat.

The second move is the entire difficulty, and it is Lesson 2's cheat in new clothes. If you only push down, the model finds that it can set the complaint to zero everywhere. Every future then fits every past, the loss sits at its floor, and the model learned nothing. Lesson 2 called this representation collapse. Here it is a flat energy landscape. It is the same failure from a different angle.

There are two families of cure. Take the picture first.

Picture it Contrastive methods are whack-a-mole. You point at specific wrong pairs — this past with that unrelated future — and you hammer their energy up by hand. It works. But the space of wrong answers is astronomically large, so you need an astronomical number of hammer blows, and the number you need grows badly as the dimensions grow.

Regularised methods are a waterbed. You never point at a wrong pair. You cap the total volume of the space that is allowed to sit low. Press one region down, and the rest must rise on its own, because there is only so much "low" to go around. LeCun prefers this family. It is the route that JEPA takes.
🗣️ Plain: two ways to stop the model calling everything compatible — hammer down named bad examples, or cap how much of the space is allowed to look good. STATcontrastive estimation vs. a capacity/volume penalty on the fitted score surface
CS/MLcontrastive methods (negative samples) vs. regularised methods (information/volume constraints, e.g. VICReg-style terms)

5 · Where this sits in LeCun's argument

Energy is what makes this course one argument instead of three facts. The world model of Lesson 1, the JEPA boxes of Lesson 2, the collapse problem, and the planning machinery still ahead are all descriptions of one energy landscape and how it is sculpted. When a JEPA trains, the prediction error in representation space is the energy. When an agent later plans, it searches for the action sequence with the lowest energy. Same number, two jobs.

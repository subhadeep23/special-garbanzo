What is a world model — and why not predict the pixels?

The one idea LeCun's whole vision hangs from. No math. Just the hook everything else clips onto.

1 · The everyday version

Right now, without thinking, you know that if you knock the glass on your desk it will fall, spill, maybe shatter. You didn't run an experiment. You have a little simulator in your head that predicts what happens next in the world. LeCun calls this a world model, and his claim is simple but heavy:

An intelligent agent — human, animal, or machine — needs a world model so it can imagine the consequences of its actions before acting. Planning is just running that simulator forward.

Babies build theirs by watching. Nobody hands an infant a labelled dataset. They observe, the world reveals what happens next, and the prediction error teaches them. That "learn by watching, with no labels" idea has a name.

🗣️ Plain: learning from raw observation by hiding part of it and predicting the hidden part — the data is its own answer key. STATthe target is a held-out part of the observation, not an external label
CS/MLself-supervised learning (SSL)

2 · The trap: predicting everything

Here's the obvious first idea for building a machine world model: show it video, and train it to predict the next frame, pixel by pixel. This is the generative approach — and LeCun argues it is the wrong goal.

Why? Picture a video of a pen balanced on its tip, about to fall. It will fall — but left or right? The exact way it topples, the precise swirl of a coffee pour, the exact leaves that flutter — these are unpredictable in detail. A pixel-predictor is graded on getting all that detail right, so it burns its capacity trying to predict noise it can never nail. It either fails, or hedges into a blurry average.

🗣️ Plain: trying to predict the future in full detail — every pixel — when most of that detail is genuinely unpredictable. STATmodelling the full conditional distribution p(x_future | x_past) in the original high-dimensional data space
CS/MLgenerative / reconstruction-based prediction
The pivot — hold onto this LeCun's move: don't predict the pixels. Predict an abstract summary. Predict "the pen falls over" — not the exact final photograph. Throw away the detail you can't predict, and only predict what's predictable. That abstract summary has a name too.
🗣️ Plain: a compact, abstract summary that keeps what matters and discards the noise. STATa set of derived features / latent variables
CS/MLa representation, or embedding — a vector z in latent space

That single swap — predict in representation space, not pixel space — is the seed of JEPA, which is Lesson 2. Everything else (energy-based models, the collapse problem, the full cognitive architecture) grows out of this one decision.

3 · Why this matters for LeCun's LLM critique

A large language model predicts the next token in full detail — it's a generative predictor, just over words instead of pixels. LeCun's bet is that this is fine for text but a dead-end for understanding the physical world, because the world's future is far too rich to predict token-by-token. That's the argument you're building toward being able to make yourself — and defend.

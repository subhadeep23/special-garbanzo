JEPA — predict the representation, not the pixels

Lesson 1 gave you the pivot. This lesson makes it concrete: the actual shape of the machine LeCun proposes, and exactly how it sidesteps the blurry average.

1 · Recap the pivot — and the blur we're escaping

Last lesson ended on one move: don't predict the pixels, predict an abstract summary. You now know why pixel-prediction fails — a model graded on squared pixel error is forced to output the average of all possible futures, and when several futures are possible (the pen falls left or right) that average is a ghost that matches none of them. The blur.

🗣️ Plain: the safest single guess, when you're graded on squared error, is the average of everything that might happen. STATthe conditional mean E[x_future | x_past] is the minimiser of expected squared error — and it's a blur whenever that conditional distribution is multi-modal
CS/MLMSE reconstruction loss collapses a multi-modal target onto its mean

JEPA's whole trick is to change what gets graded. Grade the guess on an abstract summary instead of on pixels, and the unpredictable detail can simply be left out of the summary — so there's nothing left to average, and nothing to blur.

2 · The shape of the machine

Take two chunks of an observation — call them x and y. For a video, x is what you've seen so far and y is what comes next. For an image, x is the visible part and y is a patch you've hidden. JEPA wires up three pieces:

  • An encoder for x — squashes the visible part into an abstract summary vector, s_x.
  • An encoder for y — squashes the target part into its own abstract summary vector, s_y. This is the crucial one.
  • A predictor — looks at s_x and tries to guess s_y. The training signal is simply: how close was the guess to s_y?

Read the name off the picture. Both inputs are turned into embeddings — that's the Joint Embedding. One embedding is used to predict the other — that's Predictive. It's a blueprint for a family of models — an Architecture. Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture. JEPA.

🗣️ Plain: the little machine that reads raw input and boils it down to its abstract summary vector. STATa learned map from high-dimensional data to a lower-dimensional feature vector
CS/MLan encoder — a neural network mapping input x to an embedding s = enc(x)
🗣️ Plain: summarise both pieces, then predict one summary from the other — never touching the raw pixels in the prediction. STATmodel the relationship between two latent representations, not between the raw observations
CS/MLJoint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA): predict s_y from s_x in latent space

3 · Why this dodges the blur

Here's the payoff, and it's worth sitting with. In pixel-prediction, the model was forced to reproduce every detail of y — including the pen's left-or-right that it could never know. In JEPA the target isn't y itself, it's s_y — y's summary. And a summary is allowed to throw the unpredictable part away.

The summary can encode "a pen, now fallen, lying flat" and simply not carry which direction it fell. If the target vector never records the coin-flip, the predictor is never graded on it — so it never has to average over it, and the blur never arises. You only ever predict what's predictable. That is the entire idea, delivered.

The one-sentence version to memorise JEPA predicts the representation of the future from the representation of the past — so the unpredictable detail can be discarded by the encoder instead of blurred by the predictor.

4 · The catch waiting for us (Lesson 5's hook)

One uncomfortable question should be nagging you. If the goal is just "make s_x's guess match s_y," what stops the machine from cheating — making every summary the same constant vector? Then the guess is always trivially perfect, and the model has learned nothing. That failure has a name, and it is the central problem in training these models.

🗣️ Plain: the model cheats by making all summaries identical, so matching is trivial and it has learned nothing. STATthe representation degenerates to a constant — zero variance, no information retained
CS/MLrepresentation collapse — the trivial solution JEPA training must actively prevent

How JEPA blocks that collapse — without ever decoding back to pixels — is the thread running through the next few lessons (energy-based models, then collapse itself). For now, just hold the danger in view: a predictor rewarded only for agreement will happily agree on nothing.

5 · Where this sits in LeCun's argument

An LLM predicts the next token in full detail and it works, because text is discrete and comparatively low-entropy. JEPA is LeCun's bet for the physical world, where the future is too rich to spell out: predict in the space of abstractions, not the space of observations. I-JEPA (images) and V-JEPA (video) are the first real machines built on exactly the three-box picture from §2.

The whole agent — six modules, two modes

Four lessons built one part: the world model. This lesson puts it inside the machine LeCun actually proposes, and shows that "thinking before acting" is just Lesson 3's energy, minimised over actions instead of over futures.

1 · Six boxes, one job each

LeCun's paper doesn't stop at "predict the representation." It sketches a whole agent, and every box has exactly one job. Plain words first:

Module Its one job
Perceptionturn raw senses into a summary of the current state — this is Lesson 2's encoder
World modelpredict the next state's summary, given a state and an action — this is the JEPA predictor
Costoutput one number: the agent's "discomfort" in a state
Actorpropose actions, and improve them
Short-term memoryremember recent states and what they cost
Configuratorthe manager: picks the current sub-goal and tunes the cost module for it

2 · The cost module has two halves — and one is locked

Picture it Your body has pain and hunger. You didn't learn them and you can't argue your way out of them. On top of that, you have experience: "if I touch that stove, it will hurt soon." The first is fixed at birth. The second is learned, and its job is to forecast the first.
  • Intrinsic cost — hard-wired, not trainable. It defines the agent's basic nature: its "pain" and "hunger". Because it cannot be trained, the agent cannot learn its way into wanting something bad.
  • Critic — trainable. Its job is to predict future values of the intrinsic cost, so the agent can feel a consequence before it arrives.
🗣️ Plain: a fixed sense of "this hurts" plus a learned forecast of "this will hurt later". STATa fixed loss function, plus a fitted regression model of that loss's expected future value
CS/MLintrinsic cost (immutable) + trainable critic — the critic plays the role a value function plays in reinforcement learning

3 · Two ways to act

Now the payoff. The same machine can run in two modes.

  • Mode-1 — reflex. Perception gives the state; a policy inside the actor maps it straight to an action. One pass, no imagining. Fast and cheap. This is catching a ball.
  • Mode-2 — deliberation. The actor proposes a whole sequence of actions. The world model imagines the states each one leads to. The cost module scores every imagined state. The actor tweaks the plan to lower the total, and repeats. Only when it has settled does it perform the first action — then it looks again and replans. This is planning a route across town.

If this sounds like Kahneman's System 1 and System 2 — fast intuition versus slow reasoning — that is the intended parallel.

4 · Planning is energy minimisation — over actions

Picture it A satnav doesn't try a route by driving it. It imagines the route, adds up the delay on every stretch, then nudges the route and adds up again, keeping whichever total is smallest. It only tells you the first turn. At the next junction, it does it all again.
total cost = C(s[1]) + C(s[2]) + … + C(s[T])
  • s[t]the world model's imagined summary of the state at step t. Step 0 is "now", from perception.
  • C( … )the cost module: takes one state, returns one discomfort number (intrinsic cost + critic).
  • + … +add the discomfort of every imagined step together.
  • Thow many steps ahead the agent imagines — the planning horizon.
  • =the whole plan gets one score. Each s[t] depends on the actions chosen, so changing actions changes this number.

The actor's task: choose actions a[0] … a[T] that make this total as small as possible. Because every module is differentiable, the actor can follow the gradient of the total with respect to the actions — the same tool that trains the network, pointed at the plan instead of the weights.

Cashing in Lesson 3 "Same number, two jobs." In training, you lower the energy by changing the weights. In planning, you lower the energy by changing the actions, with the weights held fixed. LeCun calls the second one reasoning: reasoning is energy minimisation with respect to action sequences.
🗣️ Plain: imagine a plan, score it, improve it, take only the first step, then plan again. STAToptimisation of a sum of stage-wise losses over a decision sequence — closely related to sequential decision theory
CS/MLmodel-predictive control (MPC) with a learned world model; "Mode-2" planning; inference by optimisation

5 · Why two modes, and how they talk

Mode-2 is expensive: the agent can deliberate over only one hard thing at a time. So LeCun proposes that the good plans Mode-2 discovers are used to train the Mode-1 policy. Over time the deliberate plan becomes a reflex. That is his account of how a skill is learned: slow and effortful at first, automatic later — like driving.

And here is the sharpest contrast with today's LLMs, which is the mission's opening question: an LLM produces each token in a single forward pass — structurally, a Mode-1 machine. LeCun's agent has a place where it can search before it commits.
